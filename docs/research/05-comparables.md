# 05 — Comparable portfolio-to-hire stories and community programs

**Research date:** 12 November 2026, afternoon Africa/Lagos.  
First-party "I was hired because I built X" write-ups are scarce. Each story is labeled with the strength of the evidence. Absence of a write-up is not evidence that a project did not help.

## A. People who joined Supabase DevRel

### Jon Meyers — teacher / engineer, short Prisma stint, then Supabase

- **Evidence:** first-party interview, January 2023. [https://ajcwebdev.com/podcasts/supabase-devrel-jon-meyers/](https://ajcwebdev.com/podcasts/supabase-devrel-jon-meyers/)

He remains on the official author roster as Developer Advocate: [https://supabase.com/blog/authors/jonmeyers_io](https://supabase.com/blog/authors/jonmeyers_io), and in `humans.txt` as "Jon M".

### What these Supabase stories do *and* do not show

**Stated or reasonably documented:** people now in the function have been teachers, SDK/docs contributors, and/or public technical creators. At least one early hire (Meyers) got in without a large audience, by showing he could already teach and ship. At least two 2025 hires (Rome, Meech-Ward) are publicly associated with pre-existing content audiences — association, not a confirmed causal hiring story.

**Not documented:** a clean, official "I built a public Supabase lab / tutorial and that is why they hired me" case. That absence is a gap, not proof that such a path would fail.

Audience size helped Robinson but was modest by later-creator standards (he cited 2,300 subscribers in 2020). Meyers did not claim a large audience.

## C. Supabase community programs and what they reward

### SupaSquad (official advocate program)

Official page: [https://supabase.com/open-source/contributing/supasquad](https://supabase.com/open-source/contributing/supasquad)

Stated tracks (members can join one or more):

- **Contributor** — help developers, improve docs, answer questions, keep spaces welcoming.
- **Content Creator** — "videos, blogs, tutorials, and other content that educates and inspires developers."
- **Trusted Host** — run meetups.
- **Event Speaker** — speak at developer events.

Stated benefits: Discord badge and Reddit flair; early feature access and feedback channel; direct team access; partner deals; exclusive swag; and, explicitly, **"Room to grow from volunteer to paid contributor to paid employee. Your path in the Supabase ecosystem."**

**Stated, and important:** the official program itself names a path from community volunteer to employee. It does not describe the selection bar or cite people who took that path into DevRel.

An older community essay called SupaSquad "unofficial" ([https://blog.hijabicoder.dev/the-supabase-influence-on-my-devrel-mental-model](https://blog.hijabicoder.dev/the-supabase-influence-on-my-devrel-mental-model)); the official page above supersedes that status as of this fetch.

Meetup / apply links (community site): [https://meetups.supabase.com/](https://meetups.supabase.com/), [https://meetups.supabase.com/apply](https://meetups.supabase.com/apply).

Jon Meyers (2023 podcast, cited above) said each Launch Week has been followed by a community hackathon, and that **Made with Supabase** itself was a hackathon winner that became the submission hub.

**What official winner write-ups actually reward (stated in the posts, not inferred as a hidden rubric):**

- A working, demoable project using Supabase (often plus a newly launched feature).
- Categories explicitly include overall quality, AI use, fun, technical impressiveness, and visual polish.
- Prizes are swag / hardware, **not a job offer**. This pass found **no official statement** that a hackathon win led to a DevRel (or other) hire.

**Inference:** hackathon write-ups celebrate shipped, polished, often playful or AI-adjacent apps with a clear demo. They are a weak proxy for *hiring* criteria, and a stronger proxy for *what the DevRel and community orgs like to celebrate in public*.

## D. Limits of this section

Portfolio-to-hire is poorly documented in public at the exact companies requested. The honest takeaway is narrower than a list of "templates":

1. Official Supabase DevRel posting asks for shipped full-stack work, remixable guides, and especially video, with audience as a bonus.
2. Official SupaSquad copy explicitly mentions volunteer → paid employee as a possible path.
3. Documented hire narratives that do exist emphasize **already-public teaching and building**, not a private portfolio PDF.
4. A single, well-sourced "this repo got me a DevRel job at Supabase / Neon / Convex / Clerk / PlanetScale / Prisma" story was **not** found in this pass.
